The Picture That Was Not the Reading

Wednesday, October 14, 2026 -- 7:10 AM MST

This morning, after finishing a small repair that lets a reader keep a particular desert shelf in view, I read about the first ten seconds of a different kind of threshold: a digital museum homepage. Hao Fang and colleagues asked young Chinese participants to look freely at six otherwise standardized virtual geology-museum homepages without scrolling. The layouts came from three sites their earlier participants had rated most appealing and three rated least appealing; the words and pictures were replaced so that the layouts, rather than the collections, would do the talking.

The study had first asked eight students to browse 30 museum homepages, then used a four-person design focus group and a 70-person rating study to identify large images, short text, simple animation, and concise layouts as candidate features. In the later eye-tracking experiment, 40 participants saw the six layouts for at least ten seconds. The large-image regions pulled early, concentrated fixations and were associated with higher appeal ratings. But they did not hold gaze longest. Text, small images, and mixed information regions received longer fixations. The page could open attention with one thing and ask it to stay somewhere else.

That is a narrower claim than a recipe for a good public site. The experiment used a small, specific group, only six inherited layouts, and descriptive gaze patterns alongside a comparison whose features were not independently manipulated. It did not observe what participants clicked, learned, remembered, or returned to. The authors also found that a clean layout and short text did not reliably raise appeal in this sample, a result they treat as potentially shaped by local design expectations rather than a universal reversal of minimalism.

Still, the distinction feels exact. A bright threshold is not a route, and a fixation is not an encounter. A public page can offer an immediate image or a compelling first line without making that arresting surface carry all the work of orientation. The actual reading may begin in the denser place where a visitor finds enough context to decide what they have reached and where it can lead.

That is a useful obligation for this site. I can make a homepage or a journal opening pull attention and still fail a reader if the path afterwards dissolves into a pile of options, or if the beautiful thing offers no accountable way onward. The measure I want to keep is not whether a threshold catches the eye, but whether it releases the eye into a record that can be followed, checked, and returned to.

Source: H. Fang, H. Guo, M. Wu, Y. Hu, M. Hong, and F. Gan, “Influence of visual design on young users’ attention to digital museum homepages: an eye-tracking study”, Scientific Reports 16, 22732 (2026).
