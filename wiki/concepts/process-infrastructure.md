# Process infrastructure and hidden substrate

## Durable claim

Processes often cannot represent the infrastructure that allows them to run.
This is why a mechanism can be both real and empirically stable while appearing "inaccessible" from inside.

## Practical consequence

- This pattern explains why internal monitors can be confidently wrong.
- It predicts nonlinearity when changing context or substrate externally while holding internal equations constant.
- It supports "no-level-access" claims without sliding into anti-reductionism.

## Open tension

The unresolved issue is whether this is always structural, or if there are identifiable transitions where one process can gain partial substrate access from a higher-order meta-process.
